## Summary

Cordova argues that Brazil's public AI push leans too heavily on frontier models, supercomputing and centralised data infrastructure. She makes the case for an "edge layer": small, specialised models running on local hardware for industrial and agricultural uses such as defect detection and predictive maintenance. Her claims are that hyperscale builds create mostly temporary construction jobs and concentrate value, that edge deployments need less energy, data transfer and cloud spend, and that Brazil's large repair workforce could be formalised to support them. She also criticises tax regimes that favour approved data-centre projects over small integrators.

## What it lets valorai measure

Supports a comparison of centralised versus edge deployment on permanent-job yield, resource intensity and local value capture, and shows how public incentive design shifts who bears indirect costs.

## Caveats / limits

Opinion and policy advocacy, not an empirical study. Headline figures (FGV jobs split, repair-sector size, small-data training claims) are second-hand and need checking against primary sources. Edge hardware still carries supply-chain, firmware and maintenance costs that the piece treats mainly as opportunity. No cost model or ROI is offered.
